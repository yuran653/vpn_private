Personal VPS/Thailand

One private road from your phone to the internet, by way of Bangkok

A VLESS + XTLS‑Reality server on an Ubuntu VPS. To anyone watching the wire it looks like a visit to Samsung's website. Your traffic leaves through Cloudflare WARP in Thailand, and the server closes every leak it can control: DNS, IPv6, fingerprinting and panel discovery.

02Step by step

What happens to one request

1 · Device

Looks like a Chrome visit

Happ opens TCP 443 to the VPS and sends a TLS 1.3 hello with Chrome's fingerprint and the name www.samsung.com. Your traffic rides inside, split by XTLS‑Vision so its shape does not stand out.

2 · Reality

Key or cover site

The server checks the hello for the client's short ID and X25519 key. With it, the tunnel opens. Without it, the connection is passed through to the real Samsung site, valid certificate and all.

3 · Routing

Three rules

  • Port 53 to any address → answered by the server's own DNS
  • Any IPv6 destination → blocked
  • Everything else → warp, the only outbound
4 · Exit

Out through Bangkok

Xray runs WireGuard to WARP in user space, IPv4 only, MTU 1280. Cloudflare's Bangkok site is 1.4 ms away. Sites see a WARP address placed in Thailand, so YouTube Music works.

04Leak guards

Each way you could be exposed, and what closes it

DNS leak

Every lookup answered inside

A query to port 53 of any address, even one with no resolver, is caught and answered by the server. The server asks over DNS‑over‑HTTPS (1.1.1.1, 8.8.8.8) through WARP.

R‑vpn‑server‑03
IPv6 egress

IPv6 does not exist here

Turned off in the kernel with ipv6.disable=1 from the first moment of boot. IPv6 destinations are blocked and AAAA lookups come back empty.

R‑vpn‑server‑02 · ADR‑0004
DPI fingerprinting

Indistinguishable from HTTPS

Reality borrows a real site's TLS 1.3 handshake, so there is no certificate of its own to spot. Probes get the real site.

R‑vpn‑server‑04 · ADR‑0003
Panel discovery

Panel on loopback only

3x‑ui listens on 127.0.0.1 and is reached through ssh -L. Only SSH and 443 are open to the internet.

R‑vpn‑server‑05 · ADR‑0002
Exit through the VPS address

Fails closed

There is no direct outbound. If WARP is down, requests fail. They never fall back to the server's address, which Google places in Myanmar.

R‑vpn‑server‑12 · ADR‑0008
Activity record

Nothing to hand over

Xray's access log and DNS log are off. Keys, tokens and the client link sit in root‑only files and never appear in script output.

05The host

A small, closed, self‑patching box

  • Firewallufw denies all incoming except SSH and 443/tcp.
  • Flood limitsPort 443: 1000 open connections or 100 new per second (burst 300) per address. SSH: 10 open or 30 per minute. SYN cookies on, backlog 4096.
  • SSHKeys only, no root, no passwords. Unauthenticated connections drop after 30 s. fail2ban watches the rest.
  • Updatesunattended‑upgrades installs security fixes and reboots at 04:00 when one needs it.
  • SpeedBBR with fq pacing, 16 MB socket buffers, no slow start after idle. Tuned for the long path to your device.
